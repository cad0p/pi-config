One evidence pack, three verification paths. Each object verifies without contacting Aqta.

An open receipt format, a wallet-held credential and a verdict anyone can check without Aqta: a reusable pattern, not a closed Aqta account workflow. The record formats have two separately written reference implementations, TypeScript and Python, which agree on all 67 conformance vectors. One implementation defines a format. 2Docket: the question being askedAfter step 1

The question is hashed into what you sign, so the verdict can never drift from what was asked.

fixed before anyone signs

3Review and countersignAfter step 2

You sign a verdict bound to the receipt and the fixed question, using a credential from your own wallet. The receipt is untouched: the verdict is a new artefact that references it.

The disclosed credential proves

  • ✓ control of a verified email address
  • ✗ not an employer
  • ✗ not reviewer authority

The attribute you disclose is stored with your verdict, and anyone holding the docket link can read it without an account. That is deliberate: an anonymous verdict proves nothing. While countersigning is in pilot, disclose the sample email credential rather than anything you would not want stored. What we keep and how to have it erased.

Which credential establishes authority is the open question, and this wallet’s own production scheme already carries two answers: pbdf.signicat.kvkTradeRegister, which discloses an organisation, its KVK number, a job title and whether the holder may act for it, and pbdf.nuts.agb, which proves a registered healthcare provider. Neither is wired in yet. We would rather test one with a reviewer who holds it than accept it untested.

What this proves, and what it does not

          

            
Proves
Seal signed the stated record, its signed fields have not changed,
              and a party holding a credential Aqta did not issue reviewed it and signed a verdict, at a time
              attested by the Yivi timestamp service, a clock Aqta does not run.

            
Does not prove
That the record matches the computation that actually ran.
              A signature is an assertion by a key holder, not evidence of execution fidelity, so a countersigned
              review certifies a review of the artefact. Closing that is open research.

            
Does not prove
That no decision was left out. A decision never written down
              leaves no trace, so a reviewer sees what they were shown and cannot conclude the rest was
              irrelevant. Completeness is the hardest of these and we do not claim it.

            
Does not prove
That the decision was correct, fair or free of discrimination.
              None of that is in the artefact. This establishes what was decided and under which rules, which is
              what lets someone competent argue about whether it should have been.

            
Credential
A verified email is a placeholder: it shows a reviewer is
              identifiable, not qualified or free of conflict. Which credential establishes authority is the
              open question, and we would rather work on it with people who issue credentials than guess.
              Professional accreditation, an organisational role attestation, something EUDI-issued once it
              exists: all plausible, none settled. If you work on credential schemes,
              [email protected].
